# Ravensmoor Calls Itself a School

Status: Text and publication time approved by the author. Queued for scheduled publication; server activation still requires deployment and Cron setup.

Scheduled publication: January 20, 2027, 9:00 a.m. Eastern, ahead of the tentative January 27 release of A Vow of Iron and Blood.

Description: Enter Ravensmoor Academy, the coastal dark academia setting of A Vow of Iron and Blood, where Old Blood houses turn education and debt into instruments of control.

---

Ravensmoor Academy sits above a storm-battered coast. Its causeway disappears beneath the tide. The Vaultspire rises high enough to be visible from the sea before the rest of the grounds comes into view.

It is a school with classrooms, a Great Hall, and rules about which parts of the building its students may enter. Those ordinary functions give the place a vocabulary for explaining its authority. They also make useful cover for the claims the Old Blood houses hold over the people brought there.

*A Vow of Iron and Blood* begins the numbered sequence of *The Blood and Bracer Chronicles*. Wren Ashcombe enters this world trying to stay close enough to protect her brother. The agreement that lets her do so binds her to House Thornecroft, and brings Isolde Thornecroft into her life as the woman who holds her contract.

This introduction stays with the academy's setting and the pressures present at the start of the novel. The sealed tower's discoveries belong to the book.

## A Causeway with a Closing Time

The route into Ravensmoor is subject to the tide. The causeway keeper will not let anyone begin crossing after the tide-bell's second toll.

That gives the coast a practical role. A person who wants to leave has to consider the time, the water, and whoever controls permission to cross. A return journey can become impossible for a while without anybody needing to lock a gate.

The buildings share that atmosphere. Salt and old iron reach through the corridors. Damp lingers in the lower halls despite the fires. Older wings depend on candlelight, and the Great Hall's ceiling rises beyond what that light can comfortably show.

Living there means adapting to conditions that are already established. The academy does not need to change its routines to make a newcomer feel small.

## The Vaultspire Divides Access

The tower's lower floors contain rooms the school uses openly. Its upper floors are sealed, with access reserved for Old Blood house heads and the Headmaster.

The same building can therefore offer instruction to students while withholding rooms from them. They know where the tower is. They can see its place in the academy. Knowing it exists does not give them a right to ask what happens in every part of it.

That arrangement makes access a form of power before any secret is discovered. Someone's ability to enter a room says something about her position. The people excluded have to live with decisions made behind its threshold.

## Education under a House Claim

Ravensmoor was ostensibly founded as neutral ground after a war between vampire houses and witch covens. Its institutional language still has uses for peace, education, and protection.

The present academy operates within a house system that conscripts witches and binds their labor through debt. The Old Blood houses have wealth and authority. Witches provide the blood magic those houses cannot cast themselves.

That dependency does not give the witches an equal place at the table. Instead, the houses have built ways to claim the people whose work they need. A student can be receiving instruction while also being prepared for obligations she did not freely choose.

Wren's attempt to protect her brother brings that arrangement close. A decision made for his safety changes what House Thornecroft can demand of her. Her reasons matter to her; they do not make the contract less useful to the people holding it.

## Isolde Holds the Contract

The romance begins inside that unequal position. Isolde has authority over Wren that extends beyond whether the two women like each other.

Wren's hatred and attraction can coexist without resolving what Isolde's control means. Genuine desire does not cancel a coercive arrangement. Nor does the possibility of devotion make every use of power benevolent.

That is one of the pressures the novel develops through their behavior. What does Isolde do when she could invoke the contract? What can Wren risk saying to somebody who holds it? What would have to change for the relationship to occupy different terms?

The answers need time and consequence. The school gives both women plenty of opportunities to discover how much their circumstances have taught them to expect from each other.

## Entering the Numbered Series

*A Vow of Iron and Blood* is Book One of a sequential series. Its story carries forward into the next numbered book; it is a different reading structure from the separate central romances of *The Monstrous Beloved*.

The standalone novella *A Compact of Salt and Iron* offers another entrance to the same world through Larke and Cassia, outside the academy. It does not need to be read first.

Ravensmoor brings the house system into a place that calls its authority an education. Wren arrives because someone she loves has been drawn into it. Staying close enough to help him means learning what the institution expects her to give up, and how much she is willing to let it take.
